Follow your curiosity
• So, specific knowledge is found much more by pursuing your innate talents, your genuine curiosity, and your passion.

Use permissionless leverage
• The most interesting and the most important form of leverage is this idea of products that have no marginal cost of replication. This is the new form of leverage.

• For labor leverage, somebody has to decide to follow you. For capital leverage, somebody has to give you money to invest or to turn into a product.

These two pages distill Silicon Valley investor Naval Ravikant's core wealth philosophy on building leverage. ### 1. Three Pillars of Getting Paid at Scale
Naval breaks "Get paid at scale" into three progressive questions:
* Accountability — answers "Why you?": Stepping up publicly to own outcomes with your personal reputation on the line.
* Leverage — answers "At scale?": Using external tools or resources to multiply a single unit of your effort.
* Specific Knowledge — answers "Why you instead of someone else?": Possessing a unique capability others cannot easily copy.

On Specific Knowledge, the notes make a counterintuitive claim: it cannot be taught, yet it can be learned.
* Beware standardized training: If a skill can be taught in a classroom or bootcamp, anyone else can be trained for it too and replace you.
* Follow innate talent and genuine curiosity: Don't chase whatever job is hottest or whatever field investors hype; follow your natural curiosity and passion.
* Live at the edge of knowledge: Specific knowledge sits in frontier or hard-to-crack areas—if you aren't 100% into it, someone who truly is 100% into it will outperform you.

### 2. Categories of Leverage & the Power of "Permissionless Leverage"
We live in an age of nearly infinite leverage, and all great fortunes come from leverage. Naval splits it into traditional permissioned levers (labor: people willing to follow you; capital: investors willing to fund you) and new permissionless levers (code and media with zero marginal cost of replication—writing code, books, podcasts, tweets, videos).

1. Commanding an "army of robots" with code: Once a developer writes software, an army of robots works through the night while they sleep. Coding is a superpower because it lets you speak their language.
2. The "magic combination" of all three levers: Tech startups explode by pairing minimum, highest-output labor (engineers, designers, product builders) with capital for marketing and scaling, plus massive code, media, podcasts, and content.

### 3. The Path from Zero: From Accountability to Judgment
1. Take accountability first: Unlike leverage and specific knowledge, accountability is immediate—volunteer for what nobody wants to own and put your neck on the chopping block.
